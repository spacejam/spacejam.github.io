Why four poles and a feedback path define an era of synthesis

Introduction

Every synthesist knows what a filter does in practice — turn the cutoff knob and the sound gets brighter or darker. But why does adding resonance change the character so fundamentally?

The Moog ladder filter uses four identical one-pole RC stages in series, with a global feedback path from the output back to the input. This topology is what gives it its characteristic sound.

The Four-Pole Topology

A single RC low-pass stage attenuates frequencies above its cutoff at 6 dB per octave. Four in series gives 24 dB per octave — steep enough to dramatically reshape a waveform.

The feedback path is what creates resonance: energy at the cutoff frequency is fed back in phase, causing the filter to self-reinforce at that frequency.

Self-Oscillation

At maximum resonance, the feedback loop sustains indefinitely — the filter becomes a sine wave oscillator at its own cutoff frequency. This is not an artifact but a physical consequence of the topology.
